Part IV

Mechanisms

The mechanisms

Each row is detailed, with its Lean evidence, on the slides listed. The storage bound is stated over the delivery model (LeanDag/DoS/) and reads no property of the rule.

Liveness, and the role of time

  • The commit rule counts references; it does not refer to time.
  • Liveness is reduced to two facts about the DAG, from some round on:
    • every reliable validator has a block in each round;
    • each reliable block references every reliable block of the round below.
  • These follow from view convergence and the pacemaker's rules.
Uses: view convergence and the pacemaker's rules (slide 69).
Gives: the support's precondition live (slide 61).

The two facts are PopulatedOn (LeanDag/Common/Participation.lean:62) and SynchronisedOn (:80). The pacemaker is PaceCore (LeanDag/Mysticeti/ViewPace.lean:114).

Mysticeti's route: certifiesAt_of_synchronisedOn (LeanDag/Mysticeti/Liveness.lean:95) gives a direct commit; Support.live (LeanDag/Properties/Derived/LeaderCommits.lean:40).

The pacemaker's rules

  • Advance: a validator holding blocks of n − f creators at round n moves past round n.
  • Catch up: after GST, a validator that holds a reliable block of round n enters round n within proc, the time to process and build a block.
  • Wait: a validator spends at least timeout(n) in round n.
  • Reference: a validator's block references every block of the previous round that it holds.

PaceCore.advances (LeanDag/Mysticeti/ViewPace.lean:171), catchup (:182), ViewPace.waits (:349: built v n + timeout n ≤ built v (n+1)), references (:351).

Drift collapses in one round

  • Validators' clocks may drift apart before GST.
  • After GST, catching up brings every reliable validator within Δ + proc of the others in one round, whatever the spread before.
  • A timeout of 2Δ + proc therefore suffices; it does not depend on the spread before GST.

PaceCore.drift_collapse (ViewPace.lean:289), driftOn_of_catchup (:306, 428); synchronisedOn_of_converges (:461), directCommit_of_wait (:708), decided_of_wait (:721), each with 2·delay + proc ≤ timeout n.

Witness ugrowLag (LeanDagTest/Reactive/Collapse.lean:65: gst = 12, delay = 2, proc = 1, timeout = 5); ugrowLag_collapse (:208), ugrowLag_decided (:230, no spread hypothesis). v0 and v1 enter every round at the same time, so their lines coincide.

(informal) "Waiting alone does not reduce drift" has no Lean statement.

The reactive schedule, and local liveness

  • A validator waits only until it holds the leader's block, then votes; the timeout is a fallback.
  • The vote round then takes at most drift + delivery + processing. When that sum is below the timeout, no timeout fires.
  • Every reliable validator decides from the blocks it holds, by an explicit time.

Chain quality

  • Coverage: the history of every committed block contains, at every round below it, blocks of at least |Correct| − f correct validators. No timing assumption is needed.
  • Inclusion: a correct validator's block is committed once a later slot it leads commits.
  • Coverage holds for every rule except Bluestreak. Inclusion holds for the seven rules with SelfParent and NoEquiv.
Uses: Quorate and CommitsCandidate (coverage); SelfParent, NoEquiv and LeaderCommits (inclusion).
Gives: lower bounds on which correct validators' blocks enter the ledger.

Equivocation and exposure

  • Safety requires no restriction on equivocation; bounded storage does.
  • A creator is exposed in a block's history when the history holds two of its blocks in one round.
  • Rule: never reference a block whose creator is exposed in your own history.
  • This alone bounds a history, but with a constant that grows like f^f: one history can hold 2^e chains of one creator.
Uses: no property of the rule; only which blocks a validator accepts.
Gives: a bound on what a correct validator stores (next slide).

The novelty budget

  • Accept a block only if its history adds at most τ blocks you do not hold; reference only blocks you have accepted.
  • The rule is independent of a block's creator.
  • Storage then grows linearly: with C correct validators, a correct validator holds at most C·((1 + fτ)·n + 1 + f) blocks at round n. At C = 3, f = 1, τ = 3, round 9: 114 blocks.

After a round R, a correct block's novelty is at most f·κ + 1 (card_novelty_le_of_byzBudget, Novelty.lean:432).

The cut

  • Each validator keeps a horizon G and discards every block below it. Validators do not agree on G.
  • Keep the blocks of rounds ≥ G and renumber round G as round 0, with no references. The result is again a valid DAG.
  • Slot d + k of the full DAG and slot k of the cut DAG have the same verdict.
Uses: Banded, Agree, and Law 1 of the support. Gives: the same verdicts and liveness after the cut, and constant storage (next slide).

Storage is constant

With the horizon at most Λ rounds below the current round, a correct validator keeps at most C·(Λ + 1) + C·f + Λ·C·f·κ blocks, whatever the round. Here κ bounds the number of new blocks that accepting one Byzantine block adds to the store. At C = 3, f = 1, κ = 3, Λ = 5: 66 blocks.

card_retained_le (LeanDag/GC/Window.lean:189), under ByzBudget D κ, RefsAccepted D, G ≤ t ≤ G + Λ. C is the number of correct validators. At the slide's values: 3·6 + 3·1 + 5·3·1·3 = 18 + 3 + 45 = 66.

Joining from an attested base

  • A new validator adopts the round-G blocks that are reached from blocks of at least f + 1 distinct creators at a later round t.
  • One of those creators is correct, so every base block is attested by a correct validator. No signatures are needed.
  • It then copies a correct peer's blocks above G. Its verdicts equal those of any validator with the full history.

Crash recovery: the message

  • A validator v₁ crashed after its block B₁ and returns at round r. It cannot create a block: a valid block needs its own previous block one round below.
  • It sends one message: B₁, r, a donor validator v₂, and fresh identifiers.
  • Every receiver computes a filled block for v₁ at each missed round: the donor's references and payload at that round, plus a reference to v₁'s previous block.
Uses: Banded (through Persist), Agree, SkipsUnsupported, Law 1. Gives: v₁ producing again, with every earlier verdict unchanged.

GapData.hgap (LeanDag/SafeSkip/Data.lean:49); SkipData (:30–73); SkipMsg (LeanDag/SafeSkip/Basic.lean:52); SkipData.fillBlock (Data.lean:159); Integration.fill_refs_eq (LeanDag/Integration/CommonTarget.lean:74).

(informal) "Rebuilding costs one round trip per missed round".

What the fill guarantees

  1. The filled DAG is valid, and every old block is unchanged.
  2. v₁ has a block at every missed round, so it can produce again.
  3. A slot led by v₁ in the missed rounds is skipped at once, given a quorum one round above (the prompt skip). No later view can commit it.
  4. Every verdict reached before the fill agrees with every verdict after it.

Jumping rounds

  • A validator's own blocks form a chain, so the donor's whole line is fixed by its top block.
  • The fill depends only on B₁, the donor's top block B₂, and the fresh identifiers. The message carries only these.
  • A receiver that holds B₂ holds the donor's whole line. After the one message, v₁ has a block at every round up to B₂'s round, so it can produce at the next.

(informal) "Constant size": fresh is an identifier supply.

Limits of the fill

  • With a horizon Λ rounds below, recovery in one message works for outages of at most Λ rounds; for longer ones B₁ has been discarded.
  • No old block references a filled block, so v₁ is not covered in the rounds it missed.
  • The storage rule must be re-checked; with a donor line whose blocks all reach B₁, and v₁ not equivocating, it is one reachability check per round.
  • If the donor line is taken from blocks every receiver already holds, nothing else needs to be sent.

Re-genesis: recovering after a long outage

  • If all of v₁'s blocks are below the horizon, its chain of own blocks cannot be continued.
  • Each validator adds a round-0 block for v₁, with no references, to its cut DAG. This needs no exception to the self-parent rule.
  • Procedure: (1) join from the attested base to read; (2) re-genesis to write; (3) one fill message from the genesis block to the current round.
  • While a quorum still produces blocks, at most f validators can be in this state at once.
Uses: Banded, Agree, Law 1, and the attested base (slide 78). Gives: a validator back in production after any outage.

The chain fill (Bluestreak)

  • Copying a donor's references would attribute to v₁ claims it never made.
  • Each filled block references only v₁'s previous block and carries no tag and no claim. The message is (v₁, B₁, r); no donor.
  • Verdicts do not change: slot 2 is committed and slot 3 skipped, before and after.
Uses: the same properties as the fill, and Bluestreak's claim discipline.
Gives: recovery for a rule whose blocks carry claims.
