Part I

Foundations

Scope

  • Protocols. Thirteen commit rules from the literature. Eleven are proved safe and live; two are shown unsafe or not live by counterexamples.
  • Properties. One small interface — four properties and a “support” — that each of the eleven rules is proved to satisfy.
  • Mechanisms. A pacemaker, chain quality, a storage bound, garbage collection, crash recovery, adaptive leaders and an adaptive leader count, checkpoints: each proved once against the interface, and composable.
  • Safety theorems use no assumption about the network.
  • The eleven rules with a carrier (DagRule): mysticetiRule (LeanDag/Mysticeti/Properties.lean:52), nemoRule (LeanDag/Nemo/Carrier.lean:37), odontocetiRule (LeanDag/Odontoceti/Carrier.lean:32), hybridRule (LeanDag/Hybrid/Carrier.lean:29), finWhaleRule (LeanDag/FinWhale/Carrier.lean:39), Hydrozoan.rule (LeanDag/Hydrozoan/Helpers/Carrier.lean:27), optimalRule (LeanDag/OptimalHydrozoan/Carrier.lean:42), bluestreakRule (LeanDag/Bluestreak/Carrier.lean:35), mahiMahiRule (LeanDag/MahiMahi/Carrier.lean:28), asyncBlueBottleRule (LeanDag/AsyncBlueBottle/Carrier.lean:26), steelheadRule (LeanDag/Steelhead/Properties.lean:41).
  • The two refuted rules: LeanDagTest/Minnow/Safety.lean, LeanDagTest/BlackMarlin/Divergence.lean.
  • Safety without network hypotheses: Properties.Safe (LeanDag/Properties/Arcs/Headline.lean:46), AnchoredRule.decided_agree (LeanDag/Common/Anchored.lean:483).
  • “Live” means progress for all eleven; four of them do not prove inclusion (slide 64).

The problem: replicated agreement

  • n validators each keep a copy of one ordered log (a ledger).
  • Clients submit transactions to any validator.
  • Safety: no two validators output conflicting logs.
  • Liveness: every transaction from a correct client is eventually in every correct validator’s log.
  • Some validators may be faulty. The protocol must tolerate them without identifying them.
(informal) Background for the audience. In Lean the ledger is commitSeq and ledgerSet (LeanDag/Common/Ledger.lean), and agreement of ledgers is AnchoredRule.commitSeq_agree, ledgerSet_agree (LeanDag/Common/Anchored.lean:521, 528).

Faults and quorums

  • A Byzantine validator may deviate arbitrarily from the protocol: send false messages, send none, or send different messages to different validators (equivocate).
  • A crashed validator stops and never resumes.
  • With n ≥ 3f + 1 validators, at most f Byzantine, a quorum is n − f validators.
  • Two quorums share at least n − 2f ≥ f + 1 validators, so at least one correct validator.
  • A correct validator does not equivocate, so two quorums cannot support two conflicting statements.
  • Faults (LeanDag/Common/Validators.lean:23): 3 * f + 1 ≤ n, byzantine.card ≤ f. Quorum quorumCard = n − f (Validators.lean:39).
  • Intersection: exists_correct_mem_inter (LeanDag/Common/Validators.lean:152).
  • Correct is the complement of byzantine; a crashed validator is in Correct. Liveness theorems name the validators that keep producing separately.

What the network is assumed to do

  • Messages can be delayed arbitrarily, up to an unknown time GST (global stabilisation time).
  • A reliable validator is a correct one that keeps producing blocks. Liveness assumes a quorum of them.
  • After GST, what one reliable validator holds reaches every reliable validator within Δ. This is called view convergence.
  • Safety is proved with no assumption about the network.
  • Liveness is proved from view convergence and the protocol’s own rules.
  • (informal) Partial synchrony is background.
  • View convergence: ConvergesWithin (LeanDag/Mysticeti/ViewPace.lean:61); PaceCore.converges (ViewPace.lean:166): gst ≤ t → holds w t ⊆ holds v (t + delay) for v, w ∈ T. T is a quorum-sized reliable set.
  • ViewPace also carries protocol rules (P7, P9), which are the validators’ behaviour, not the network.

Blocks and the DAG

  • Validators work in rounds. In each round each validator creates one block: a round number, its creator, references to earlier blocks, and a payload of transactions.
  • A valid block at round r > 0 references blocks of round r − 1 from at least n − f distinct creators, one of them its own creator.
  • The blocks and references form a directed acyclic graph (DAG). A block’s history is every block it reaches by references. A validator’s view is the set of blocks it holds, including their histories.
  • Blocks are named by identifier (a hash), so two validators may hold different sets of blocks but agree on what each identifier means.
  • Block (LeanDag/Common/Block.lean:20); ValidWrt (Block.lean:61) has four clauses: predecessor (references at r − 1), distinct creators, quorum, self-parent (some block by the creator).
  • Identifiers: BlockRecord.block (LeanDag/Common/BlockRecord.lean:35).
  • History: Reaches, historyFrom (LeanDag/Common/CausalHistory.lean, LeanDag/Common/History.lean). A view is closed under references (viewComplete, LeanDag/Properties/Carrier.lean:49).
  • Some rules change validity: Nemo drops self-parent and distinct creators and uses a majority (LeanDag/Nemo/Basic.lean:48); FinWhale replaces self-parent by leader exclusion (LeanDag/FinWhale/Model/Rule.lean:27–37); Bluestreak requires a quorum only of leader blocks (LeanDag/Bluestreak/Rule.lean:45, 79).

Certified and uncertified DAGs

  • In a certified DAG (Narwhal, Bullshark), each block receives a certificate, built by an extra exchange of signatures, before others may reference it.
  • In an uncertified DAG (Mysticeti and this family), no certificate is sent.
  • A block two rounds above a leader is a certificate for it when its references include n − f blocks that reference the leader.
  • Every direct commit rule is then a threshold on a set of blocks found in the graph.
  • (informal) Narwhal and Bullshark.
  • Certifies / CarriesVotes (LeanDag/Mysticeti/Model/Rule.lean:36; LeanDag/Common/Support.lean:324). Threshold shapes: LeanDag/Common/Rules.lean.
  • On the witness: Certifies U3 8 0 (LeanDagTest/Mysticeti/Model.lean:261).

Slots and the leader schedule

  • A slot k has a round and a leader. The schedule may put several leaders in one round and a leader in every round.
  • The candidates of slot k are the blocks of its leader at its round. A correct leader has at most one candidate; a Byzantine leader may have several.
  • Each slot is decided as commit (one candidate) or skip.
  • The ledger is the committed leaders in slot order, each followed by the blocks in its history not yet output.
  • Slots (LeanDag/Common/Slots.lean): slotRound, leader, a kind (default 0, :44), keyed by (round, leader) (:40).
  • Pipelined and two-leader schedules: pipeSlots, duoSlots (LeanDagTest/Mysticeti/Pipelined.lean:53).
  • IsLeaderBlock (LeanDag/Common/Leader.lean:28). A Byzantine leader with two candidates: witness U6 (LeanDagTest/Mysticeti/Model.lean).
  • Ledger: commitSeq, ledgerSet, OutputAt (LeanDag/Common/Ledger.lean).

One decision relation for every rule

A slot is decided in one of four ways:

  1. Direct commit: the rule’s commit condition holds in the validator’s view.
  2. Direct skip: the rule’s skip condition holds.
  3. Indirect commit: a later committed slot (the anchor) links a candidate.
  4. Indirect skip: the anchor links no candidate.

The anchor is the nearest eligible committed slot above; every eligible slot in between must be skipped.

  • AnchoredRule.Decided (LeanDag/Common/Anchored.lean:189). The “in between” premise is hmid.
  • The relation is inductive, not a function. The order of questions in the flowchart is for presentation.

A rule is a card of thresholds

Every rule fills in the same record:

  • the wave: how many rounds above the proposal the direct rules read;
  • the commit and skip conditions;
  • the link from an anchor, possibly in several graded rungs;
  • a tie-break between linked candidates.

Agreement and the agreement of ledgers are proved once, for every rule whose card satisfies a short list of laws.

  • structure AnchoredRule (LeanDag/Common/Anchored.lean:86): waveAt (the direct rules read up to round r + waveAt, as a function of the slot’s kind), Commit, Skip, rungs, Link, tie, Anchor.
  • AnchoredRule.Laws (Anchored.lean:292) is a property of the rule relative to a record invariant. Proved once: decided_agree (:483), decided_mono (:502), commitSeq_agree, ledgerSet_agree, outputAt_agree (:521, 528).
  • Card values: coreAnchored (LeanDag/Mysticeti/Model/Rule.lean:86–95).

Eligibility

  • An anchor at slot j is eligible for slot k when round(k) + wave < round(j).
  • A slot one round above k cannot reach a certificate for k’s leader. A slot two rounds above reaches one only if its own block is a certificate.
  • Anchoring at such slots would let one validator’s direct commit become another validator’s indirect skip.
  • EligibleAt (LeanDag/Common/Anchored.lean:37).
  • ¬ EligibleAt 2 0 1, ¬ EligibleAt 2 0 2, EligibleAt 2 0 3 (LeanDagTest/Mysticeti/Pipelined.lean:72, 77, 80); the commit-to-skip hazard is described at Pipelined.lean:68–72.
  • Reaches is reflexive (LeanDag/Common/CausalHistory.lean:35), which is why a round-r+2 anchor that is itself a certificate does link.
  • Pipelined.lean defines the schedule but no DAG; the figure places pipeSlots leaders (k mod 4) on the Ugrow 6 blocks.

A skip must count blocks

  • The absence of the leader’s block from a view is not a sound reason to skip: a validator cannot distinguish a silent leader from a leader whose block has not yet arrived.
  • In Mysticeti a direct skip needs n − f blocks of the round above that reference no candidate. Those blocks exist, and later blocks do not alter them.
  • blameSkip q, slotBlamers (LeanDag/Common/Leader.lean:99).
  • ugrow_not_directSkip (LeanDagTest/Mysticeti/Growth.lean:308) covers Ugrow N with N < 3k; the left figure is Ugrow 5, slot 2. ugrow_directCommit (:272) gives the commit in Ugrow 8; ugrow_commits_recur (:332); fairSlots (:260–265).
  • Other rules skip differently (Nemo never skips directly; FinWhale, Hydrozoan, Bluestreak use other counts); their slides say so.

How the results are checked

  • Every definition and theorem is written in Lean 4 and checked by its kernel.
  • A witness is a small concrete DAG, usually 4 to 9 validators, on which Lean computes the verdicts. Witnesses show that hypotheses can be met and that bounds are tight; counterexamples show that a published claim fails.
  • Every DAG in this deck is drawn from a witness. Figures that are not are marked as diagrams.
