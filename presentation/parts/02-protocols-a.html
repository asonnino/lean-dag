Mysticeti: direct commit and direct skip

n = 4, f = 1, q = 3. Slot 0 is led by v0 (0, L); another slot by v3 (3).

  • At round 1, 4 5 6 are votes for 0; v3's block 7 does not reference it. 3 ≥ q
  • At round 2, 8 9 10 each reference those three votes: three certificates. 3 ≥ q: commit.
  • At round 1, 4 5 6 reference no block of v3 at round 0: three blames. 3 ≥ q: v3's slot is skipped.
U3 (LeanDagTest/Mysticeti/Model.lean:112–127); Certifies U3 8 0 (:261); DirectCommit U3 0 0 (:264); blames U3 3 1 = {0, 1, 2} (:258); DirectSkip U3 3 0 (:267). A direct commit and a direct skip exclude each other: not_directCommit_of_directSkip (LeanDag/Mysticeti/Rule.lean:67).

Mysticeti: indirect commit

  • At round 2 only v0's block 8 is a certificate for L. One certificate is fewer than q, and one blame is fewer than q: no direct decision.
  • The slot at round 3, led by v0 (12), is eligible. It is committed directly by rounds 4 and 5.
  • Its block references v0's certificate 8, so L is committed indirectly.
U7 (LeanDagTest/Mysticeti/Model.lean:470–495, 526): certificates U7 0 0 = {8}, DirectCommitIn U7 V7 12 3. Why an indirect skip cannot contradict a direct commit: a direct commit puts a certificate in the history of every block from round r + 3, exists_certificate_reaches_of_directCommit (LeanDag/Mysticeti/Rule.lean:79); indirect_agrees_with_direct (:185).

Mysticeti: why it is safe

  1. Two quorums share a correct validator.
  2. A correct validator votes once per round, so a slot has at most one certified candidate.
  3. A direct commit puts a certificate in every history from round r + 3.
  4. A direct skip leaves no certificate anywhere.
  5. So any two views decide every slot the same way, and their ledgers agree.

No step uses the network. Every rule that follows is proved safe by the same five steps, with its own counts: these are the laws of slide 10.

Nemo-Nemo: the rules on a crash

n = 3, majority 2. Validator 2 creates blocks in rounds 0 and 1, then crashes.

  • Slot 3 (round 3, leader v0, 8): v0 and v1 vote at round 4. 2 ≥ 2: commit.
  • Slot 2 (round 2, leader v2) has no candidate. It has no direct skip; the anchor 11 at slot 4 links nothing for it: indirect skip. Slot 3 is not eligible for slot 2.

Odontoceti: direct and indirect

n = 6, f = 1: direct threshold 5, indirect threshold 3. v0 is Byzantine.

  • Slot 0 (leader v0, 0): 5 blames, 5 ≥ 5: direct skip.
  • Slot 1: 3 votes, 3 blames: no direct decision. The anchor 21 at slot 3 has voters {1, 2, 3} in its history, 3 ≥ 3: indirect commit.

Odontoceti: two twins pass

  • A Byzantine leader can make two blocks (twins, 0 and 6) in one round.
  • Both twins can reach the n − 3f threshold inside one anchor's history.
  • If each validator committed any passing twin, two validators could commit different twins.
  • The rule therefore commits the least passing candidate (16: commit), an order every validator computes the same way.
Witness Utwin6, utwin6_both_pass (LeanDagTest/Odontoceti/Model.lean:250–260). Block 19 is not the slot's committed anchor; at the actual anchor (slot 2, block 15) only twin 0 passes, and utwin6_slot0 commits it (:268–273). Hybrid

  • fb Byzantine validators and fc crash-prone validators. A crash-prone validator never equivocates but may stop.
  • Quorum q = n − fb − fc. The rule is Odontoceti's: q votes to commit, q blames to skip, the least candidate with k voters in the anchor's history to link.
  • k must satisfy 2fb + fc + 1 ≤ k and k + 3fb + 2fc ≤ n. Such a k exists exactly when n ≥ 5fb + 3fc + 1.
  • With fc = 0 the thresholds are Odontoceti's.

FinWhale

  • Committee n = 3f + 2p − 1, with 1 ≤ p ≤ f. Safety: up to f Byzantine. Fast path: when at most p are Byzantine.
  • Three thresholds: slow 2f + p, block validity n − f, fast n − p.
  • Fast commit at round r + 1: n − p votes.
  • Slow commit at round r + 2: 2f + p blocks, each referencing 2f + p votes.
  • Validity: if two of a block's references reference two different blocks of v, the block may not reference a block of v (leader exclusion).
  • p = 1 gives n = 3f + 1; p = f gives n = 5f − 1.

FinWhale: fast and slow commit

f = 2, p = 2, n = 9; validators 0 and 1 are Byzantine.

  • Fast. Seven round-1 blocks reference the leader's block 0: 7 ≥ 7, commit at round 1.
  • Slow. Every round-2 block references 7 votes; six such blocks suffice: 6 ≥ 6 certificates, commit at round 2.

FinWhale: skip and evidence

  • A round-r+2 block is evidence for l when it references at least f + p − 1 votes for l; if its references show the leader equivocating, at least f + p for l and at most f + p − 1 for every other candidate.
  • Direct skip: 2f + p blocks at r + 1 reference no candidate, and 2f + p blocks at r + 2 are evidence for nothing.
  • Indirect commit: the anchor's history holds a slow certificate for l, or 2f + p evidence blocks for l. The least such l is committed.
  • Evidence is needed because a fast commit may leave no slow certificate.

FinWhale: what the formalisation found

  • The paper's counting argument for Lemma 22 covers only p = 1. For p ≥ 2 (smallest case n = 9) the lemma is proved by a pigeonhole argument.
  • In the paper's case C3 of Lemmas 18 and 19 the count has no margin at p = 1; the lemmas are proved instead by induction on the time a block is built.
  • The paper's proofs of Lemmas 6 and 7 pass through the slow skip, which does not apply when a validator holds no block of the slot; they are proved through the evidence quorum.
  • The paper's latency claim (Definition 1) is proved, and the evidence count is exactly tight at n = 3f + 2p − 1 and one short at n − 1.
