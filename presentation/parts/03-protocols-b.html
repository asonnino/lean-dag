Hydrozoan

  • f Byzantine, c crashed, a slack k ≥ 0: n ≥ 3f + 2c + k + 1.
  • Thresholds: p = ⌊(c + k)/2⌋, q = n − f − c, qFast = n − p, qCert = ⌊(n + f)/2⌋ + 1, qSlow = 2f + c + 1, qWeak = f + p + 1.
  • Fast commit: qFast votes at r + 1. Slow commit: qSlow certificates at r + 2, each referencing qCert votes.
  • Skip: qFast blames at r + 1.
  • Indirect, two rungs: (1) a certificate in the anchor’s history; (2) if no candidate has one, the least candidate with qWeak votes in the anchor’s history.

Hydrozoan: why the second rung exists

n = 7; v0 is Byzantine and equivocates; v1 has crashed.

  • The leader receives exactly qFast = 6 votes: fast commit.
  • No block is a certificate: each round-2 block references only 4 votes, one fewer than qCert = 5.
  • A validator that missed the fast commit consults the anchor. Rung 1 finds no certificate. Rung 2 finds 4 voters, at least qWeak = 3: commit.
  • U4, V4 (lk4, 32 blocks; LeanDagTest/Hydrozoan/DirectSafety.lean): FastCommit U4 2 0, certificates U4 2 0 = ∅, supporters U4 2 1 = {0, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}; the anchor reaches 4 voters (checked by evaluation; the witness’s docstring states the same). V4 withholds voter 13, so every direct rule fails in view.

Hydrozoan: what the formalisation found

  • The six threshold inequalities hold for every slack k ≥ 0.
  • One of them must be strengthened to qFast + q − n − f ≥ qWeak, so that every eligible anchor sees at least qWeak votes of every fast commit.
  • Round-robin by slot is not fair inside the bound: at n = 5, f = 0, c = 2, it need not place three live leaders consecutively. A rotation aligned to waves is fair.

Bluestreak

  • n ≥ 3f + 1, quorum n − f.
  • Sparse DAG: only leader blocks must reference n − f blocks. Other blocks reference their own previous block and, optionally, leader blocks.
  • A round-r+2 block claims L by naming it in its payload, or by referencing n − f votes for it.
  • Direct commit: n − f claims for a leader-tagged L.
  • Direct skip: n − f round-r+1 blocks in view, and for each candidate n − f of them that omit it.
  • Indirect: a claim for a tagged L in the anchor’s history.
Compared with Mysticeti: most blocks reference two blocks instead of n − f; a certificate is replaced by a claim, which only names the block.
Far fewer references per block. Three rounds at n = 3f + 1.
Blocks need not reference a quorum, so chain-quality coverage is not shown. Claims require a discipline (slide 39).

Bluestreak: an anchor must be committed

  • A claim names a block; the votes behind it need not be in the claimer’s history.

Bluestreak: the claim discipline

  • From round 2, a correct block’s claim must name a block certified exactly two rounds below.
  • Safety, liveness and every mechanism assume this discipline.
  • Read two rounds deep, the discipline is preserved by garbage collection.

Mahi-Mahi

  • n ≥ 3f + 1. A wave of w rounds: propose at r, vote at r + w − 2, certify at r + w − 1. At w = 3 this is Mysticeti.
  • A block votes for the least block of the leader’s round found in its history (not only among its direct references).
  • A direct skip blames the slot: the blamer has no block of the slot in its history.
  • The leader of a wave is revealed only after the wave, so the adversary cannot direct its message scheduling against it.
Compared with Mysticeti: a longer wave, votes found anywhere in the history, a skip per slot, and an unpredictable leader.
Live with no timing assumption about the network.
More rounds per commit; it needs an unpredictable leader (a common coin).

Mahi-Mahi: votes and what a wave commits

  • At w ≥ 4 a history can contain two twins of the leader; a block votes only for the least.
  • At w ≥ 4, once a correct quorum produces through the wave, some correct validator’s block is directly committed.
  • At w ≥ 5, at least n − f − b correct validators are good: their block would commit if chosen (b = number of Byzantine).
  • An adversary that knows the leader in advance can prevent the leader’s block from entering the other validators’ histories.
  • twin4: twins 0 and 4 lie in block 8’s history; 8 votes for 0.
  • aim4: block 5 enters no other history up to round 3. Good at w = 4: {0, 2, 3} (shaded); at w = 5: all.

Mahi-Mahi: what the formalisation found

  • The paper counts all n − f validators in the common core as good. Twins of Byzantine leaders can split the votes, so the proved count is n − f − b.
  • Proved commit probability per wave, at w ≥ 5: at least (n − f − b)/n, which is always at least 1/3. At n = 4, f = b = 1: 1/2.
  • 2f + 1 leader slots per round guarantee a commit at w ≥ 5.
  • Mysticeti skips per candidate; Mahi-Mahi skips per slot.

Async BlueBottle

  • n ≥ 5f + 1. Propose at r; vote and decide at r + 2, with votes found in the history as in Mahi-Mahi; no certificates.
  • Direct commit: n − f votes. Direct skip: n − f blames.
  • Indirect: n − 3f voters in the anchor’s history; the least passing candidate is committed.
  • The leader is unpredictable, as in Mahi-Mahi.
Compared with Odontoceti: the vote round moves from r + 1 to r + 2 and votes are found anywhere in the history, so that the rule is live with an unpredictable leader.
Live with no timing assumption, three rounds per wave; commit probability at least (n − 3f)/n per wave.

Steelhead

  • Each slot has a kind: synchronous (a known leader, short wave ws) or asynchronous (an unpredictable leader, long wave wa).
  • A slot is decided by the rule of its kind, at its own wave; its anchor lies at or above round + w(kind).
  • A period P sets the kinds: round r is asynchronous when r mod P = 0. The period is adapted at intervals of I rounds.
  • Two instances: Mysticeti with Mahi-Mahi (n ≥ 3f + 1, waves 3 and wa ≥ 4), and Odontoceti with Async BlueBottle (n ≥ 5f + 1, waves 2 and 3).
Compared with Mysticeti: two rules on one DAG, chosen per slot, and a controller that chooses how often the asynchronous rule runs.
Short waves while the network is synchronous; liveness under asynchrony.
Two rules, a coin, a period controller, and a failover (slide 47).

Steelhead: the stall

  • At any period P ≥ ws, an adversary delivers each synchronous leader’s block to exactly f + 1 validators. Neither a commit nor a skip quorum forms.
  • The synchronous slot just below each asynchronous round is then undecided in every view, and the output does not advance beyond it.
  • A committed asynchronous slot does not decide the synchronous slots below it.
  • At period 1 every slot is proved to be decided.
  • Stall (LeanDag/Steelhead/MahiMahiPair/Liveness/Statement.lean:611): slots at rounds ≡ P − 1 (mod P) are never decided, under “no synchronous certificate and no synchronous direct skip”. AllDecidedBelowAtPeriodOne (:639). Nothing is stated for period 2.
  • st20 (LeanDagTest/Steelhead/Counterexamples/Stall.lean:70, 101–117, st20_slot0 :129, st20_stall_view :170): candidates 6, 11, 12 have voters {9, 10}, {14, 15}, {16, 17}; round 4 certifies block 1. Validator 0 is Byzantine.
  • rt_stall (LeanDagTest/Steelhead/Counterexamples/RotatingStall.lean:270).

Steelhead: control and failover

  • The period is changed by a second verdict, read at the asynchronous control slots of each interval.
  • Failover: when the anchor is more than I rounds above the last committed output, the next interval runs at period 1.
  • Under failover a stalled slot is decided almost surely. The probability it is still undecided after M blocks, each of wa·K coins, is at most 2 · ((nwa·K − (n − f − b)wa·K) / nwa·K)⌊M/2⌋, where b is the number of Byzantine validators and K bounds the period.
  • ControlDecided, controlRound, controlSlots (LeanDag/Steelhead/Model/Chain.lean:79–129); PeriodAt.anchor (Model/Period.lean:144); PeriodOne (LeanDag/Steelhead/MahiMahiPair/Period/Statement.lean:277).
  • UndecidedTail (LeanDag/Steelhead/MahiMahiPair/Coin/Statement.lean:277), badBlockBound (:265); K is the period bound; hypotheses 5 ≤ wa, K ≤ I, wa ≤ I, wa·K ≤ q·I; DecidedAlmostSurely (:461).

Steelhead: what the formalisation found

  • The paper’s period update alone retains a stalled period; liveness requires the failover.
  • An equivocating leader at a slot’s anchor floor can hold the slot undecided.
  • The interval must satisfy I ≥ 2·maxPeriod and I ≥ maxPeriod + wa − 2.
  • In the paper’s timeout appendix, with exactly one vote missing, a certificate forms with probability f/n, not (n − f)/n.
